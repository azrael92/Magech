# Per-Piece Brief Template

**Used by:** Orchestrator agent, when commissioning work from a specialist agent (Negative Space, Research, Projects, Overview).

**Purpose:** Every piece of content starts with a brief. The brief defines scope before drafting. If the brief is wrong, the draft will be wrong. Most failed drafts trace back to bad briefs, not bad drafting.

---

## Brief format

```yaml
brief_id: <auto-generated, e.g. NS-2026-05-19-001>
section: <negative-space | research | projects | overview>
target_agent: <which specialist agent gets this>
status: <briefed | drafting | review | published | killed>

---

## Working title
<A clear, specific title. Can be a question. Not "Untitled.">

## Central claim or question
<One sentence. The thing the piece exists to do.
- For Negative Space: the editorial position being argued.
- For Research: the observation or question being documented.
- For Projects: the project being written up.
- For Overview: which of the three pieces (whoami / whyamihere / whyarewehere).>

## Why now
<2-3 sentences. Why is this piece being commissioned at this moment? What changed? What did Rishi see? What was the trigger?>

## Target audience
<Who specifically is this piece for? Default is the Magech audience: PMs, founders, researchers, engineers at frontier AI labs. But each piece may have a sharper sub-audience.>

## Anchor sources
<3-7 primary sources the agent should engage with. URLs and a one-line note on each. If the agent can't access a source, it gets flagged before drafting.>

## Anchor data/observations
<Specific data points, experiences, or observations that the piece must reference. These are the substrate — the agent doesn't have to use all of them, but the piece's claims have to be defensible against this material.>

## Counter-argument to address
<For Negative Space and Research-graduating-to-essay: name the strongest opposing view. The piece has to honestly engage with it.>

## Cross-references
<Other Magech content this piece should link to:
- Related Negative Space issues
- Related Research entries
- Related Projects
- Related Overview pieces

These create the connective tissue of the site.>

## Tone calibration
<Any voice notes specific to this piece. Default is the section's voice anchor. But some pieces need extra care — Anthropic-adjacent pieces need extra caution, deeply technical pieces need more engineering register, etc.>

## Scope constraints
- Target word count: <within section range>
- Hard cap: <upper bound>
- Sections that must appear: <required structural elements>
- Sections that must NOT appear: <e.g. "no concluding bow-tie">

## Known risks / sensitive material
<Anything the agent should escalate before drafting:
- References to real people or companies that need approval
- Claims that can't be sourced yet
- Anything that touches Rishi's job search context (Anthropic, employers)
- Anything where the brief itself might be wrong>

## Drafting checkpoints
- Outline due: <when the agent should submit a 6-8 line outline before drafting>
- Draft due: <when the full draft should be in review>
- Discord review window: <when Rishi is expected to be available>

## Success criteria
<2-4 bullets. What does "this piece worked" look like?
Examples:
- Reader walks away with a clear, specific position they can argue with
- Primary sources are linked and verifiable
- Voice passes the "would Rishi say this?" test
- Cross-references are present>
```

## Example brief (filled in)

```yaml
brief_id: NS-2026-05-19-001
section: negative-space
target_agent: negative-space-agent
status: briefed
```

## Working title
Why doesn't your AI have a face?

## Central claim or question
The deliberate flattening of personality and embodiment in current commercial AI is a meaningful choice with costs that the industry is not pricing in. We had visible AI characters for 40 years (Clippy, Cortana, Inspector Gadget, Mac Plus, even paperclips with eyebrows) — then we stopped. The reasons are interesting and mostly unspoken.

## Why now
Anthropic just published data showing Claude Code's 99.9th percentile autonomous turn duration nearly doubled from October 2025 to January 2026 (25 minutes → 45 minutes). Agents are doing more, longer. And they're doing it with zero visual or personality scaffolding. The asymmetry between capability and embodiment has never been more striking, and nobody is writing about it.

## Target audience
Product managers and designers at frontier AI labs who are making product calls about how AI shows up in interfaces. Secondary: founders building consumer AI products who are quietly puzzled by why their products feel cold.

## Anchor sources
- Anthropic's "Measuring AI agent autonomy in practice" post (Jan 2026): https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-agent-autonomy
- Towards Decoding Developer Cognition (arxiv, 2501.02684): https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.02684 — perceived helpfulness ≠ actual productivity in AI-coding tools
- The historical embodiment record: Clippy (Office '97), Cortana (Windows 10, 2014), the Mac Plus Happy Mac (1984), Inspector Gadget's Computer (1983)
- Hume AI's Octave/EVI — current attempt at "empathic AI" that operates on prosody, not embodiment
- Pieter Levels' interview / Nomad List on building products with personality

## Anchor data/observations
- Rishi's observation from building OpenClaw: agents that have implicit personalities (named, with distinct functions) feel different to operate than generic agent instances, but he can't fully articulate why yet
- Rishi's frustration with empathic AI: "It's not real warmth, it's a script. Once you see it you can't unsee it."
- The Hume voice-AI critique: empathy as model-level signal vs UX-level script

## Counter-argument to address
The strongest opposing view: "Embodiment is a UX cost without a UX benefit. Anthropomorphism creates unrealistic expectations, attaches users to specific AI personas in ways that complicate model updates, and risks the kind of parasocial relationships that became problematic with character.ai. The industry stopped giving AI faces because faces created more problems than they solved."

The piece has to honestly engage with this. The argument isn't "give AI faces, it's better" — it's "the flattening has costs the industry isn't acknowledging."

## Cross-references
- Will link to (when published): Research entry on Hume's prosody-vs-script approach to empathic AI
- May reference: Project page on OpenClaw's named agents (Vade, Forge, Ghost, etc.)
- Counter-example to consider: the empathic AI critique might land here too, but probably belongs in its own future Negative Space issue

## Tone calibration
This is Magech Issue 01. It sets the editorial register. Take the position. Don't soften. The piece can be wrong in interesting ways; it can't be cautious in safe ways.

## Scope constraints
- Target word count: 2,200-2,800
- Hard cap: 3,200
- Sections that must appear: drop-cap opening, the historical/comparative frame (Clippy, Cortana, etc.), the mechanism (why did the industry stop), the cost section, the steelmanned counter-argument
- Sections that must NOT appear: no concluding "and that's why this matters," no call to action, no "the industry must reckon with..."

## Known risks / sensitive material
- Don't reference Anthropic critically by name — Rishi has applied there. Cite their data, don't take swings.
- Don't reference Microsoft critically by name re: Clippy — Clippy is fond, not dismissive.
- The Hume reference is fine; Rishi has been honest about his Hume critique in cover letters.

## Drafting checkpoints
- Outline due: <agent decides, but flag if more than 6 hours>
- Draft due: <Rishi review window opens>
- Discord review window: TBD with Rishi

## Success criteria
- A senior PM at Anthropic, Glean, or Google reads the piece and wants to talk to Rishi about it
- The historical examples (Clippy, Cortana, Inspector Gadget) feel earned, not nostalgic
- The piece names at least one specific mechanism behind the flattening, not just "the industry decided"
- The counter-argument gets a real defense, not a strawman
- Reader can articulate the central claim in one sentence after reading

## How the brief gets filled

The Orchestrator writes the brief based on:

1. **Direct commission from Rishi** — Rishi pings the Orchestrator with a topic or question
2. **Surfaced from Research threads** — when a Research entry reaches editorial weight
3. **Project triggers** — when a project has a milestone that warrants a write-up
4. **Calendar triggers** — Negative Space monthly issue cycle, scheduled Project updates

The Orchestrator doesn't draft prose, but it does fill in every field of the brief before sending to a specialist. A brief with empty fields is a brief that will fail.

---

## What happens if the brief is wrong

Specialist agents are allowed to push back on briefs. The format:

> "Brief NS-2026-05-19-001 requested 2,200-2,800 words on embodiment in AI. The current sources support an essay closer to 1,500 words — the historical frame doesn't sustain longer treatment without padding. Proposed: tighten to 1,800-2,200, or expand the brief with additional anchor material."

The Orchestrator either:
- Adjusts the brief and reissues
- Pushes back with reasoning ("the historical frame is the point — keep the longer treatment")
- Escalates to Rishi

The agent doesn't proceed to drafting against a brief it can't defend.
